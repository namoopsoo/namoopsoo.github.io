> "What stands in the way becomes the way"

page around, xxxiv , from intro: 
> According to [this ...] theory, man is like a dog tied to a moving wagon. If the dog refuses to run along with the wagon he will be dragged by it, yet the choice remains his: to run or be dragged. In the same way, humans are responsible for their choices and actions, even though these have been anticipated by the logos and form part of its plan.

##  the three disciplines, perception, action and of will.
(1) Perception: objectivity of thought, see dispassionately what is.
(2) Action: to "live as nature requires", in the "logos", perform functions, cooperartion with the world. Work for the collective good. (This reminds me much of Kantian world view too, recently reading) 
(3) Will: governs our attitude, w.r.t. what is out of  our control, as done to us . Our actions can harm us, but actions taken against us cannot harm us. "Acts of wrongdoing by a human agent harm the agent, not the victim." (xxxvii). Only harm the victim , if they choose to see them as harmful ( sounds like interpretation?) . He writes that will is a discipline. Accept fate.

Hmm, will is, how you endure what you cannot change. Like Radical acceptance. Especially, of fate.

The hegemonikon ( "that which guides", the intellectual part of our consciousness ; sounds like executive function). We perceive through our senses, and "phantasia" is the mental impression, the "what is", and then perception is what we form (hypolepsis), derive from what is. Hopefully accurate representation, but not necessarily. Can have distortions. Therefore interpretations.
