Going back to [1], from writing [2], i think in the conversation between DHH and Lex Fridman, they identify a sweet spot for swarms. 

## Safe side
So never using parallel agents, on the one hand, is playing it safe. You work iteratively with one agent on the task at hand. The premise is you likely dont know exactly ehat you want or necessarily what you need, at least when youre in the fog of war. For sure you have your own plan for your project, half in your head, half in  markdown and a third half you discussed with others and proceeded to forget 😭. But this is just a plan for your sanity, and would be useless to give to your agent because that would be a fool's errand. And any way, you are still multitasking, becouse you ore still juggling many commitments, but you are not using multiple harnesses within the scope of any one of them. 

## Extreme side
But there is an extreme case where you ore multitasking with multiple agents, a swarm say, because you are trying to be clever  and you break down  your project, into deliverable user stories, all with acceptance criteria and definitions of done. You write what story  on what other story, and all your stories have tshirt sizes. (Im grtting weak in the knees even writing  this! 🤮) Better yet, you didnt spend your precious time on this, you asked your favorite LLM to break down your project for you. Now you unleash your orchestrator, to work on this magnificent waterfall. us

Yea waterfall software projects  never worked in  real life and agents are not going to be any different. 

But there is a better way?!

## Goldilocks
So I think in [1], i realize DHH points to several very some uses for swarms.  One case he describes his passion to optimize e the initial install time for Omarchy , from ISO. And he describes what i can only call a kind of hyperporometer optimization focused on reducing that 1008 time. So he has several different ideas for how to do this, one is messing with the files  that need to be uncompressed ond another is doing work while the user is T thinking or reading text. I think there were other ideas too so now he has agents trying to solve the some problem (time reduction), with the some final verifiable outcome. So now parallelization makes perfect sense.

In fact he described several other scenarios that very similarly cut up the space, that are kind of like map reduce problems. I realize I described this concept too earlier .
